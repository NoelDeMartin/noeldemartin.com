I'm just getting ready to board the flight to Tokyo, and I have to say, Kumamoto was awesome! I managed to see everything I wanted, and more.

On the One Piece side; yes, I visited all 10 statues (pictures below). On the one hand, it wasn't that difficult, given that I managed to see the first 5 on the first day (Robin --> Nami --> Zoro --> Usopp --> Franky). On the other hand, it wasn't that easy either, because I had to take multiple trains, buses, and walk a fair amount (I even took a taxi!). By the time I got to Franky, it was already dark :(. Fortunately, I managed to visit it again today, just before going to the airport :).

Given the initial success, I was able to take it easy the rest of the days and mix other activities. On the second day, I visited Jinbe --> Luffy --> Chopper, and Sanji --> Brook on the third. Other than the statues, I found a couple other One Piece-related things I didn't know about. For example, in the Kumamoto Station there is a 2-side mural commemorating the [Kumamoto Revival Project](https://onepiece.fandom.com/wiki/Kumamoto_Revival_Project) (which is the reason the statues exist), and I had the privilege of riding a One Piece Shinkansen for my visit to Fukuoka. Not to mention seeing One Piece stuff basically everywhere :D.

But of course, One Piece wasn't the only reason for my visit to Kumamoto. I also came because of Miyamoto Musashi, and that didn't disappoint either. I started with the visit to Reigando Cave, but I also went to [Ganryuujima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganry%C5%AB-jima), the [Shimada Museum](https://www.shimada-museum.net/eng.html), and [Musashi's Grave](https://en.japantravel.com/kumamoto/musashizuka-park/15210). I also used these days to re-read [The Book of Five Rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Book_of_Five_Rings) (which is quite short, by the way; I would have re-read [Musashi](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musashi_(novel)>), but that's a bigger commitment). Specially in the first part (Earth), I couldn't help but wonder about how my main craft, programming, is disappearing... And then I realized that Musashi's craft, the Way of the Sword, also disappeared a long time ago. I guess everything is bound to disappear eventually, and we have to move on to newer crafts. At least I'm fortunate that I was already into product, not just coding, and I should be able to make the transition. Though I will miss typing the code myself. I wonder what Musashi would think of all of this (actually, guns already existed in his time, so the sword was already losing its value... He may have been one of those programmers who refuses to use AI 😅).

Anyway, all of these places are very much apart, and certainly not near the center or any other "hot spots". In any other situation, that could have been a problem. But in my case, I'd say this added to the experience, because it also meant that I spent most of my time in rural Japan :D. I have to say, I've lived my whole life in Spain, but in many aspects, there's nowhere I feel more at home than in Japan. All the geek stuff aside, everything is so well organized, people are so polite and helpful, and the food is so good anywhere you go. Of course, I know Japan also has its dark side (overwork, etc.), but my experience every time I've visited has been outstanding. Looking forward to enjoying the 2 weeks left!

That's it for now. If you were expecting to read something about Vue, I'm sorry that you're suddenly learning all about One Piece and Musashi 😅. I promise that my next update will be all about Vue Fes, which is now 2 days away (or not, if something comes up that takes over my next update as well 🙈, but I doubt it given that I've already spent a lot of time in Tokyo before).
